# 5 — Toward the Primary Region

Stage E. This is an **instructional progression**, not a calculation of the Primary Region. No reverse operator is used or implied. Content classes as in [Document 1](01_INTRODUCTION.md).

## 5.1 What the canonical sources say  [CANONICAL]

**The Primary Region.** "The Primary Region is the exceptional observational condition in which there is insufficient relational information to instantiate the full A→B→R path. Do not infer additional Primary Region mathematics from this statement. The Primary Δ→Σ implementation and its declared admissibility conditions are preserved exactly" (`kernel_reference.md` §10, Origin, 2026-09-29).

**Which kernel applies.** From §10:

| Condition | Kernel applied |
|---|---|
| Interior not established (M can declare the relation but cannot resolve its constituent loci) | **Primary kernel** Δ → Σ. B is not evaluated: it is not an identity operator evaluated with no effect |
| Interior established | **ABR kernel** A → B → R |

**Resolving power of M.** The observational resolution principle (`operators_notation_and_constraint.md`) separates two conditions for a declared relational phenomenon P:

- ℛ_M(P) = 1: "the observable record contains a traceable expression of P through M";
- 𝓜_M(P) = 1: "M resolves the internal mechanism of P — the constituent loci and their directed relations are independently declarable through M".

ℛ_M = 1 with 𝓜_M = 0 is *expression-observed, mechanism-unresolved*. ℛ_M = 1 with 𝓜_M = 1 is *locus-resolved*. The canonical record classifies Primary Region phenomena as expression-observed at the Primary Region and locus-resolved at larger scales. It states that "the language 'not directly observable' is inadmissible as a universal classification".

## 5.2 The progression on the ramp  [EXPLANATORY]

**Step 1: the whole set (Exercises 1 and 2).** Under this M (pencil marks, a ruler and the observed passage of a marble), P1 to P4 are each resolved as loci, and every passage between neighbouring marks is resolved as a relation. Two interior loci are established, and A → B → R applies. The relations in this set are *locus-resolved at the scale of the marks*.

**Step 2: one relation alone (Exercise 4).** Declare only P2, P3 and the relation P2 → P3. M still declares the relation, but no interior locus is established. The kernel that applies is the Primary kernel. Δ gives 45.0 mm, the same value A gives for that relation in the whole set. B and R cannot be evaluated, because the information they act on (a declared continuation) is not in this declaration. **Taking a relation out of the whole removes relational information. It does not reveal anything new about the relation.**

**Step 3: inside the relation.** Ask what lies *between* P2 and P3. Under this M, nothing there is resolved: there is no mark, no reading, and no declared relation. The relation P2 → P3 is declared, but its constituent loci are not resolved by this M. That is the wording of the §10 condition for the Primary kernel.

So, starting from the observable whole and moving inward, you reach a relation that M declares but whose interior M does not resolve. **This is as far as the ramp can take you.**

## 5.4 The boundary: where this lesson stops  [OPEN]

**A finer M moves away from the Primary condition, not toward it.** Suppose you draw a new mark Q between P2 and P3, and observe the marble passing P2 → Q → P3. Then Q is an interior locus. The finer structure is locus-resolved, and A → B → R applies to it. Increasing the locus resolution of M (𝓜_M = 1) gives more structure for the ABR kernel. It does not show what the Primary condition is like. That is why the lesson does not claim that a "magnified ramp" shows the Primary Region.

**The canonical Primary Region is a physical region outside this lesson.** In the canonical record, the Primary Region loci are particle-scale (for example the proton, electron, neutron and muon, `cross_region_energy_expression.md`). The declared measurement there is at the edge (`observable_variable_sets.md`, Primary Region: x[v] = (ε[e], q[v])). Its phenomena are expression-observed through apparatus such as track curvature in a magnetic field (`validation_record.md`). None of that apparatus, and none of those declarations, is part of Lesson 1.

**Open for Origin:**

- *Is the ramp's single relation an instance of the Primary Region, or only an instance of the Primary kernel's condition?* The canonical sources define the Primary Region as an observational condition (§10). They do not say whether a two-mark declaration in an everyday domain counts as one. The lesson calls it "the Primary condition" and makes no stronger claim (Q-R2-1).
- *What are the "augmented observational circumstances"?* The canonical sources do not define this term. The closest canonical concepts are ℛ_M and 𝓜_M: the record of a phenomenon's expression through M, and the resolution of its constituent loci through M. One possible reading is an M that establishes ℛ_M = 1 for a phenomenon whose loci it does not resolve (𝓜_M = 0). The lesson does not adopt that reading. It is returned to Origin (Q-R2-1).
- *A full demonstration would need a separate apparatus and declarations* (an M whose declared measurement is at the relation itself, and a declared observable expression to compare with). The lesson documents the boundary here and stops.

**Not done, by design:** no inverse procedure reconstructs anything inside a relation from Δ, A or R. No such operator is defined in Kernel V9, and none is introduced here.
